# Skeleton and writer's block

For a user who is starting or stuck. The skeleton is questions with empty answer slots. It contains no statements
about the user's results. Write it to a new file only when the user asks; otherwise keep it in chat.

## Start with a question or a choice

Ask for the one-sentence key message first: "What is the single claim of this paper?" If the user cannot say it in one
sentence, stay at the skeleton stage before any prose. Then offer the order options below; independent guides
disagree on order, so the choice is the user's.

## Writing-order options

| Option | Idea | Fits when |
|---|---|---|
| Outline first | Question-based outline including the figures and tables, then prose | The story is still unclear |
| Figures first | Build tables and figures, write the text around them; storyboard the order that best supports the hypothesis | The data exist and the results are the story |
| Easiest section first | Methods, then Results, then Discussion; Introduction and abstract last | Blocked at the start; common in ecology guides |
| Backwards | Main conclusion, then Results, Methods, Discussion, Introduction, Abstract, Title | The conclusion is clear but the framing is not |

Also useful: separate planning, drafting and revising; treat the first draft as disposable; talk to a colleague or
supervisor and bring a list of what you already tried.

## Question skeleton

Section by section; the user answers in their own words.

- **Key message:** What is the one-sentence claim? Which observation would change your mind?
- **Introduction:** What is the broader topic and why does it matter? What is known? What is the gap? What is the
  question or hypothesis, and what did you do to address it? (goal, obstacle, approach, result, benefit)
- **Methods:** What system or species, where and when? What was measured and how? Which analyses were planned and
  which exploratory? What would a reader need to repeat it? Ethics and permits?
- **Results:** Which figure or table answers each question? What does each show, with its estimate and uncertainty,
  before any interpretation? What was unexpected?
- **Discussion:** Which findings answer the question? How do they compare with other work? What are the limits?
  What follows from them, and what does not?
- **Abstract and title:** (written last) Which sentences of the key message belong in each?

## After the user answers

Ask follow-ups on thin or vague answers ("what number supports 'more active'?"). Do not turn the answers into
paragraphs. If the user asks for a sentence, give one labelled example from their own answer, at their hedge level.

## Ecology and behaviour notes

IMRaD is the norm in ecology and behaviour journals; formats such as combined Results and Discussion or short
communications exist, so the journal's instructions decide. For tracking-based work, the Methods questions in
[reporting-and-ethics.md](reporting-and-ethics.md) belong in the Methods part of the skeleton.
