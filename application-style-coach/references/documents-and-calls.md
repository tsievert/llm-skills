# Other application documents, and how real Finnish calls differ

All limits below are examples seen on 2026-10-30. They are time-bound and call-specific: always read the user's
actual call. The skill's own numbers never override the call.

## What real calls ask (examples)

| Aalto tenure track (2025 ad) | Single PDF; cover letter; CV with the TENK sections ("need not follow this format"); list with significant publications highlighted; research portfolio 4-10 pages; teaching portfolio per Aalto guideline; references. |
| Aalto general instructions | Only documents requested in the ad; no sensitive personal data (health, religion, political opinions, family circumstances); all material in English. |
| University of Turku tenure track (2025) | CV (e.g. TENK) with clear reporting of career breaks; numbered selected publications max 10; research plan about 3 pages; teaching portfolio; file size limits. |
| Tampere University tenure track | CV; letter of motivation max 2 pages on suitability and how the applicant would develop research and teaching. |
| Research Council of Finland, Academy Research Fellowship / Project Funding | RCF CV template (max 3 pages in the fellowship CV guideline, no images, no metrics); fellowships also a "Merits and increased competencies" narrative CV (2 pages plus 1 page for up to ten key outputs); complete list of publications as appendix; English requested; CV is a public document. |

Patterns: limits differ widely; a letter may be 1 or 2 pages; a "research plan" is 2-3 pages at some universities
and a "research portfolio" 4-10 pages at others (different documents: check whether the call wants retrospective
plus prospective, or prospective only); several calls say the CV should follow TENK, one does not mention it.

## Cover or motivation letter

Job of the letter: what the CV cannot show, namely why this position and group, the applicant's direction and the
fit. Check:

- Does it repeat the CV in prose? (Ask: what does the reader learn here that the CV does not say?)
- Does the first sentence say something specific, instead of a generic opener ("I am writing to apply...")?
- Could any paragraph be pasted into another application unchanged? Ask for the specific link to this call's
  criteria, strategy or group; the user supplies it.
- One job per paragraph, point first.
- Tone: confident, factual; Finnish academic context tends to dislike showiness (secondary source), so claims need
  facts beside them. Flag both inflation and false modesty.
- Length against the call; contact details, date, recipient and position named as the call or the university asks.
- Addressee and names spelled correctly; consistent with the CV.

## Research statement, research plan, research portfolio

First check which document the call wants: retrospective and prospective statement (past, present, future) or
prospective plan with methods and timeline. Then:

- Is the future part visible and specific (a question or aim) rather than "I will continue to study X"? Is the
  balance between past and future a conscious choice? Advice sources agree on emphasis on the future; no fixed ratio.
- Independence: could a sentence appear unchanged in the supervisor's grant? Where does the text say "I", where "we"?
  Funders ask for this explicitly (for example how the idea differs from the doctorate).
- Lead with the question or finding, not the credentials or the method; contribution sentences rather than activity
  sentences ("what did this establish?").
- Hedging sweep ("may potentially", "I hope to", "it might be interesting to"): ask whether the uncertainty is real.
- Written for a reader from another subfield: acronyms defined, jargon glossed once.
- Too many projects, too little depth; feasibility; fit with the host.
- No references are added or checked by the assistant (standing rule); the user keeps their own citation style.

## Teaching portfolio or statement

Finnish universities describe it as a first-person narrative written for the position (lengths from 2-3 pages up
to 5-10; unit instructions decide). Check:

- Written for this position and its teaching, not a general philosophy.
- Concrete examples of what was done and how it worked, plus what was learned, instead of statements of belief.
- Reflection includes development areas or next steps.
- Selected merits only (TENK heading 11); claims verifiable.
- Structure follows the university's own headings if it has them.

University guides read in full (2026-10-30; the call and unit instructions still win):

- Tampere University: 3-5 pages (Education and Culture docent guide: at most 5), five sections in this order:
  pedagogical thinking (philosophy); pedagogical training and maintaining skills; teaching and supervision
  experience; development of teaching and supervision; teaching cooperation and community-based development.
  Assessed against a five-level matrix, and an interview or demonstration can verify the claims.
- University of Jyvaskyla: 2-3 pages, written from the perspective of the position, six topics: pedagogical thinking
  and training; teaching and supervision experience (including how feedback was used); contribution to teaching
  development; teaching competence (latest trial lecture as mm/yyyy, place, purpose, grade); production of learning
  materials; other merits (awards, networks). Up to three samples, each with the reason for choosing it.
- Aalto University (2024 guide): up to 5-8 pages plus 6-10 pages of numbered appendices; narrative, first person,
  evidence for every claim, with reflection. Five parts: approach to teaching and learning (with examples and evidence
  of effect on learning); teaching experience (own role per course and thesis); curriculum development and educational
  leadership; development as a teacher (pedagogical studies with credits, strengths and development areas); feedback
  (how it was collected and used, awards). Expected appendices are tables of courses and supervised students, a
  sample syllabus and a list of pedagogical training. Tenure or lecturer track: show development since the latest
  appointment.

Extra checks these guides support (ask, do not supply the content):

- Is the philosophy justified and tied to a concrete practice or course, rather than stated alone?
- Are pedagogical studies named with scope (credits)? Tampere's matrix counts ECTS.
- Is feedback use shown (what changed because of it), not only that feedback was collected?
- Is the user's own role in development work and collaboration clear?
- Do the section order, headings and date formats match the university's guide?
- Could each claim be verified in an interview or demonstration?

## List of publications and highlighted publications

Formatting only; the assistant does not check whether publications exist or whether details are correct.

- Applicant name and list date; complete references in one style; the same status words throughout.
- Ministry classes A-I (A peer-reviewed articles, B non-refereed, C scientific books, D professional, E general
  public, F artistic, G theses, H patents, I audiovisual and ICT) when the call uses them; dissertation articles
  also under G.
- Research Council of Finland list rules: only published and accepted works (no unaccepted manuscripts), no confidential
  information, no journal metrics; other calls differ.
- "Highlighted" or "key" publications: is the short note about the user's role and why it matters for this position,
  rather than impact claims? Ask for facts (role, contribution), do not supply them.

## Package-level checks

- Every requested document present and nothing unrequested added (some calls ignore extras).
- Total page limit, file format, file naming and size as the call states.
- Same names, titles, dates and spelling across all documents.
- Public-document check: CV and list may be public; confidential data stays out.
