# Check the local-language material (Finland)

The application documents are written in English, but the requirements behind them are often more detailed in
Finnish than in the English pages and guides. Reasons seen: funder and university guides exist in Finnish only,
programme-level guides add rules (page budgets, character limits, even a required statement on AI use), and English
pages lag behind or summarise. Treat the English text as possibly incomplete.

## Standing caveat

- Before judging a document against a call, ask the user whether the call, department page or funder guide also exists
  in Finnish (or Swedish) and whether the Finnish version says more or something different.
- If the user reads Finnish, ask them to check the Finnish page for limits, headings, fonts, required statements and
  deadlines and to paste what differs. If you can fetch the page, say which language version you read. If the user
  does not read Finnish, say that you read the English text only and that details may be missing.
- Do not translate the call yourself and treat the translation as authoritative for formal requirements.
- The same applies to other countries: use the target country's language for the call and for guidance from
  universities, funders and researcher unions. The user's experience is that local-language material is often more
  informative than its English translation.

## What was found in Finnish (examples, October 2026; check the current pages)

- **Suomen Akatemia research plan structure (tutkimussuunnitelman rakenne).** Headings must not be changed or removed; the
  application cannot be submitted if the system detects page or structure errors; fonts, line spacing and whether the
  reference list counts toward the page limit differ by year and scheme; in-text references, no footnotes; "describe the
  parts as applicable according to your discipline's practice".
- **University of Helsinki doctoral programme guide (Finnish, March 2026).** Per-section character limits, evaluation
  criteria, and a section asking the applicant to state briefly how AI tools were used in preparing the application.
  The English version was not checked.
- **University of Turku programme guides.** Page and word budgets per section and a warning that not following the
  instructions can lead to rejection; plan in English if the thesis is in English.
- **Tampere programme guide.** Plan may be written in Finnish or English; methods described by principle, with
  justification, not technical detail.
- **Teaching portfolio guides in Finnish** (for example Lapland): guiding questions such as "what did I learn from it?"
  and "what feedback did I receive?", portfolio as a verifiable public description; researcher-union guidance stresses
  development as a teacher, not only excellence.
- Sources of this kind are listed with links in `research/application-style-coach-ledger.md` of the llm-skills repo
  (section 11). A known Finnish guide on the structure of a research plan (Hakkarainen 2014) was named by a Helsinki
  funding office but not read.

## How to use this in a session

1. Ask for the call text in the language the call is published in, and for any Finnish-only guide the unit points to.
2. Note differences between the two language versions in your summary of requirements (what the English text omits).
3. Coach on the English document as usual; the call's requirements, including any statement on AI use, come first.
4. Say in the closing note which language version you read.
